# Output Risks [layout: columns] [accent: output] [cols: 3]

What comes out is compromised.

### Aesthetic Homogenisation
Everything starts looking the same.

### Context Collapse
Assumes a similar, generic audience every time.

### Data Hallucination
Numbers that were never real.

# “When did you last start from a blank page, and took your time to solve something?” [layout: quote] [accent: process]

---
# Process Distortion [layout: columns] [accent: process] [cols: 4]

Your workflow gets warped.

### Anchoring
First draft is the chosen direction.

### Satisficing
Stopping once it’s good enough.

### Prompt Fixation
Focus shifts from designing to improving prompts.

### Speed–Quality Confusion
Fast and good aren’t the same thing.

---
# “If it looks right, does that mean it is right?” [layout: quote] [accent: overtrust]

---
# Overtrust [layout: columns] [accent: overtrust] [cols: 4]

You believe AI more than you should.

### Competence Illusion
Highest confidence is in areas you know the least.

### Automation Bias
Trusting machine to be always right.

### The “Looks Right” Trap
Familiar isn’t the same as accurate.

### Sycophantic Confirmation
It just tells you what you want.

---
# “If there is no AI tomorrow, what will you struggle to do?” [layout: quote] [accent: deskilling]

---
# Deskilling [layout: columns] [accent: deskilling] [cols: 3]

You lose abilities you need.

### Cognitive Offloading
Letting it think so you don’t have to.

### Skill Atrophy
Muscles you stopped using fade away.

### Creative Learned Helplessness
Forgetting you can start from nothing.

# “If AI made the mistake, who should pay the price?” [layout: quote] [accent: accountability]

---
# Accountability [layout: columns] [accent: accountability] [cols: 2]

No one is responsible for anything.

### The Black Box Excuse
“AI did it” isn’t a defence.

### Attribution Erosion
Losing track of whose idea it was.
